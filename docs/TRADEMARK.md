# Preliminary name search: "FIRE"

**This is not a legal clearance search.** It is a knockout search: enough to
tell us whether the name is obviously unusable before anybody spends money on
a domain, a logo, or paid acquisition. A real clearance opinion needs a
trademark attorney, and it should happen before the first dollar of branding
spend, not after.

Searched August 2026 against public USPTO records and third party mirrors.

## What is already on the register

| Mark | Owner | Class | Goods | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FIRE, Reg. 1608887 | AVL LIST GMBH | 9 | Simulation software for thermodynamic operations, flows, temperature and pressure curves | Registered and renewed, status date 2020 |
| FIRE, App. 90565567 | (pending applicant) | 9 | Fire extinguishers, fire alarms, evacuation devices | Non final action mailed, 2021 |
| FIRE, Reg. 4576212 | All American Brands, Inc. | 34 | Cigars | Registered |

Neither Class 9 entry is a trading product, so neither is a likely source of
consumer confusion with a market terminal. But both sit in the same class we
would file in, which makes a clean Class 9 registration for the bare word
harder rather than easier.

## The bigger problem is not the register

In finance, "FIRE" already means something else. It is the common shorthand
for Financial Independence Retire Early, a large and unrelated personal
finance movement. That matters twice over:

* **Distinctiveness.** A common English word with an established, unrelated
  meaning in the exact market we sell into is a weak mark. Weak marks are hard
  to register and harder to enforce.
* **Discoverability.** Anybody searching for the product by name lands in
  somebody else's conversation. This is a marketing cost, not a legal one, and
  it is the one that shows up in the acquisition numbers first.

## Assessment

The name is not blocked. Nothing found would stop us shipping a trading
terminal called FIRE tomorrow. What we do not get is protection: the bare word
is crowded, already registered by others in our class, and swamped in our own
market by an unrelated meaning.

## Two ways forward

**Keep FIRE, register nothing.** Use it under common law rights, spend nothing,
accept that anybody else can use it too and that a holder in an adjacent field
could eventually object. Reasonable for v1 while we find out whether the
product sells at all.

**Adopt a composite mark.** A distinctive word paired with FIRE, or FIRE with a
distinctive logo, is registrable and enforceable in a way the bare word is not.
This is the option to take if the product works and the name is going on a
domain, a Stripe statement descriptor and paid ads.

The recommendation is to ship v1 as FIRE, spend nothing on registration yet,
and get a proper clearance opinion at the same time as the legal review of the
EULA. One attorney conversation, two answers.

## Not decided here

The internal name for the private system is unrelated to any of this and stays
internal. Nothing in the customer build references it.
